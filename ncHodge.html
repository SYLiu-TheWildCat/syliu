ncHodge Structures

We start the discussion with a definition of ncHodge structures and its relation to pure Hodge structures. In [KKP08], they proposed the notion of "non-commutative Hodge structures" and constructed a candidate for non-commutative Hodge structures associated to any smooth proper dg category $\mathcal{C}$. The non-commutative Hodge structure is defined similar to Simpson [Simpson91], as a canonical holomorphic vector bundle over $\mathbb{P}^1$ with prescribed connection. Let's start with some brief review of connections of vector bundles over twistor spheres.

Meromorphic connections and holomorphic bundles.

Let $\mathbb{C}\{u\}$ be the ring of convergent holomorphic functions on $\mathbb{C}$ (in other words, the Stein algebra of $\mathbb{C}$). Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a finite dimensional $\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}]$-vector space.

Definition. A meromorphic connection $\nabla$ on $\mathcal{M}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $\nabla : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ satisfying Leibnitz law: $\nabla((fg)m) = (\nabla f)g m + f (\nabla g)m$. A holomorphic extension of $\mathcal{M}$ is a free finitely generated $\mathbb{C}\{u\}$-module $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying $\mathcal{H} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}\{u\}} \mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}] \cong \mathcal{M}$.

Note that $\mathcal{M}$ is actually a meromorphic bundle over $\mathbb{C}$ with poles at $0$, while $\mathcal{H}$ is a holomorphic vector bundle over $\mathbb{C}$.

Definition. We say $\mathcal{H}$ is logarithmic with respect to $\nabla$ if $\nabla(\mathcal{H}) \subseteq \mathcal{H} \frac{du}{u}$. We say $(\mathcal{M}, \nabla)$ has at most a regular singularity at $0$ if we can find a holomorphic extension $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{M}$ logarithmic with respect to $\nabla$.

In fact, whenever $(\mathcal{M},\nabla)$ has at most a regular singularity at $0$, we can always find a (non-canonical) holomorphic extension $\mathcal{H}$ of $\mathcal{M}$ which is a holomorphic vector bundle over $\mathbb{C}$ [PS08, Proposition 11.2].

Remark. In fact, there is a bijective correspondence between the set of algebraic vector bundles over $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$ with a regular singularity at $\infty$ and the set of finite-dimensional $\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}]$-vector spaces equipped with meromorphic connections. Therefore we can algebraically regard such data $(\mathcal{M},\nabla)$ as a vector bundle over $\mathbb{P}^1$ with log pole at $\infty$ and a possible pole at $0$.

Stokes structure.

The usual Riemann-Hilbert correspondence [PS08, Section 11.1.2] relates holomorphic/algebraic vector bundles over $\mathbb{C}$ with at most regular poles along $0$ and $\infty$ to $\mathbb{C}$-local systems on $\mathbb{G}_m = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, extendable to $\mathbb{P}^1$, by taking flat sections. When the meromorphic connection $\nabla$ has higher poles at $0$, Riemann-Hilbert correspondence fails. Instead, we should be able to see the Stokes structure, as observed and studied by Malgrange [Malgrange83]. Roughly speaking, we should regard it as a "local system under étale topology". More explicitly,

Definition. Given a meromorphic vector bundle with meromorphic connection $(\mathcal{M},\nabla)$, the sheaf of local $\nabla$-flat sections forms a locally constant sheaf $\mathbf{S}$ on $\mathbb{G}_m$ and hence $\mathbb{S}^1$, with the following filtration, called Deligne-Malgrange-Stokes filtration $\{\mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega}\}_{\omega \in \mathrm{Del}}$, defined as follows:
  • $\mathbf{Del}$ is the complex local system on $\mathbb{S}^1$ for which for every open subset $U \subseteq \mathbb{S}^1$ the space of sections $\mathbf{Del}(U)$ is defined to be the space of all holomorphic one-forms $\omega$ on the sector $Sec(U) := \{ r e^{i\phi} \mid r > 0, \phi \in U \}$ which are of the form$$\omega = \left( \sum_{a \in \mathbb{Q},\, a < -1} c_a u^a \right) du,$$ where at most finitely many $c_a \neq 0$ and the branches $u^a$ are chosen arbitrarily. Germs of sections of $\mathbf{Del}$ are naturally equipped with a total order as follows: if $\omega', \omega'' \in \mathbf{Del}(U)$ and $\phi \in U$, and if $$\omega' - \omega'' = c_a u^a + \text{(higher order terms)},$$then we say $\omega'\le_{\phi}\omega''$ iff $\mathrm{Re}\left( \frac{c_a e^{i\phi(a+1)}}{a+1} \right) < 0$.
  • For every $\phi \in \mathbb{S}^1$ and every $\omega \in \mathbf{Del}_\phi$ the stalk $(\mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega})_\phi \subset \mathbf{S}_\phi$ is defined to be the subspace $$(\mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega})_\phi := \left\{ s \in \mathbf{S}_\phi = \Gamma(\mathbb{R}_+^{\times} e^{i\phi}, M^{\mathrm{an}})^{\nabla} \;\middle|\; e^{-\int \omega} s \text{ has moderate growth in the direction } \phi,\ \text{i.e.}\ \| e^{-\int \omega} s \|_{|\mathbb{R}_+^{\times} e^{i\phi}} = O(r^{-N})\ \text{when}\ r \to 0,\ N \gg 0 \right\}$$ Here $\| \bullet \|$ is the Hermitian norm of a section of $M$ computed in some (any) meromorphic trivialization of $M^{\mathrm{an}}$ near $u=0$.
Remark. The subset $\mathbb{S}_{\leq\omega}$ for a given section $\omega$ does not form a locally constant subsheaf over $\mathbb{S}^1$, as the total order might change when we move the point $\phi$ in $\mathbb{S}^1$.

Before we move on, let's illustrate the definition a little bit. Let $K=\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}]$ be the localization and $\hat{K}$ be its completion with respect to the $u$-filtration, so that $(\mathcal{M},\nabla)$ is a free $K$-module of finite rank equipped with a connection. The assignment $t\mapsto t^p$ gives an étale cover of $\mathbb{A}^1$, and we write $L$ for the field extension of $K$ by the equation $t^p=u$. The extension $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{E}\otimes_K L$ is a vector space over $L$ together with an induced connection given by $(t\nabla_{d/dt}) (e\otimes t^k)=k(e\otimes t^k)+\frac{1}{p}((u\nabla_{d/du})e\otimes t^k)$. Let $\alpha\in\Omega^1 (L)=L\otimes dt$ be a meromorphic $1$-form on the étale cover, then we have a canonical rank $1$ $L$-vector space $\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}$ where $\nabla_{d/dt} f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} +\frac{\alpha}{dt}f$. Two such vector spaces with connections $\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}$ and $\mathcal{F}^{\beta}$ are isomorphic if and only if $\alpha-\beta$ has a simple pole at $0$ with the coefficient of $t^{-1}$ being an integer.

We then have the following formal decomposition theorem:

Theorem. Let $(\mathcal{M},\nabla)$ be a vector space with connection over $E$, then after possibly a ramification $t^p=u$, one can find a formal isomorphism $$\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\hat{K}}\hat{L}\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}\otimes_{\hat{L}}\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}),$$where the $\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}$ have the meaning given above (with $L$ replaced by $\hat{L}$), and where the $\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}$ are regular.

This provides a complete formal classification of all vector spaces over $\hat{K}$ with meromorphic connections, and the idea of classifying vector spaces over $K$ with meromorphic connections is to find lifts of the given formal decomposition. Note that for given $\theta\in\mathbb{S}^1$, the stalk $\mathbf{Del}_{\theta}\otimes_K E$ consists of meromorphic sections of $E$ in an open cone neighbourhood of $\theta$, and their formalization clearly extends to the whole of $\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\}$. Denote this map by $T$, a theorem of Hukuhara-Turrittin states that

Theorem(Hukuhara-Turrittin). Let $(E,\nabla)$ be a vector bundle with connection over $K$, then for all $\theta\in\mathbb{S}^1$, the map $T\colon\ker (\nabla,\mathbf{Del}_{\theta}\otimes_K E)\to\ker (\nabla,\hat{E})$ is surjective.

The main application of the theorem of Hukuhara-Turrittin in [Malgrange83] is that

Theorem. There is an equivalence of categories $$\left\{\text{Vector bundles over }K\text{ with connections}\right\}\xrightarrow{\Phi}\left\{\text{Stokes structures}\right\}.$$

This can be regarded as a (local) generalization of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for vector bundles with meromorphic connections.

ncHodge structure

With the previous preparations, we are now ready to abstractly define non-commutative Hodge structures.

Definition. A rational pure nc-Hodge structure consists of the data $(H, \mathcal{E}_sB, \mathbf{iso})$, where:
  • $H$ is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded algebraic vector bundle on $\mathbb{A}^1$.
  • $\mathcal{E}_B$ is a local system of finite dimensional $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces on $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$.
  • $\mathbf{iso}$ is an analytic isomorphism of holomorphic vector bundles on $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$:
    $\mathbf{iso}: \mathcal{E}_B \otimes \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}} \xrightarrow{\sim} H|_{\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}}$.

The isomorphism $\mathbf{iso}$ also induces a natural flat holomorphic connection $\nabla$ on $H|_{\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}}$.
These data have to satisfy the following axioms:

  • (nc-filtration axiom) $\nabla$ is a holomorphic connection on $H$ with a pole of order $\leq 2$ at $u=0$ and a regular singularity at $\infty$. More precisely, there exist:
    • a holomorphic frame of $H$ near $u=0$ in which$$\nabla = d + \left( \sum_{k \geq -2} A_k u^k \right) du$$with $A_k \in \mathrm{Mat}_{r \times r}(\mathbb{C})$, $r = \mathrm{rank}\, H$.
    • a meromorphic frame of $H$ near $u = \infty$ in which$$\nabla = d + \left( \sum_{k \geq -1} B_k u^{-k} \right) d(u^{-1})$$and $B_k \in \mathrm{Mat}_{r \times r}(\mathbb{C})$.
  • ($\mathbb{Q}$-structure axiom) The $\mathbb{Q}$-structure $\mathcal{E}_B$ on $(H, \nabla)$ is compatible with the Stokes data. More precisely, let $(\mathbf{S}, \{\mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega}\}_{\omega \in \mathrm{Del}})$ be the Stokes structure corresponding to the germ $(\mathcal{H}, \nabla)$, and let $\mathbf{S}_B \subset \mathbf{S}$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-structure on $\mathbf{S}$ induced from $\mathcal{E}_B$ via the isomorphism $\mathbf{iso}$. We require that the Deligne-Malgrange-Stokes filtration on $\mathbf{S}$ is defined over $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e.
    $(\mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega}, \mathbf{S}_B) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} = \mathbf{S}_{\leq \omega}$
    for all local sections $\omega \in \mathrm{Del}$.
  • (opposedness axiom) The $\mathbb{Q}$-structure $\mathbf{S}_B$ induces a real structure on $\mathbf{S}$ and hence a complex conjugation $\tau: \mathbf{S} \to \mathbf{S}$. Let $\hat{H}$ be the holomorphic bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ obtained by the gluing of $H^{\mathrm{alg}}_{|\, |u| \leq 1}$ and $(\gamma^* \overline{H^{\mathrm{alg}}})_{|\, |u| \geq 1}$ via $\tau$, where $\gamma: \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is the real structure on $\mathbb{P}^1$ fixing the unit circle, i.e. $\gamma(u) = 1/\overline{u}$, then the bundle $\hat{H}$ must be holomorphically trivial.

A morphism $\mathbf{f}\colon (H_1,\mathcal{E}_{B,1},\mathbf{iso}_1) \to (H_2,\mathcal{E}_{B,2},\mathbf{iso}_2)$ of nc-Hodge structures is a pair $\mathbf{f} =(f,f_B)$ where $f\colon H_1\to H_2$ is an algebraic map of vector bundles intertwining the connections, and $f_B\colon\mathcal{E}_{B,1}\to\mathcal{E}_{B,2}$ is a map of $\mathbb{Q}$-local systems, such that $f\circ\mathbf{iso}_1 =\mathbf{iso}_2\circ (f_B\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{O}})$. We write $(\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})$ for the category of pure nc-Hodge structures.

In order for classifying space (moduli stack) of $\mathbb{Q}$-ncHodge structures, we also make sense of variations of ncHodge structures:

Definition. Let $S$ be a complex manifold. A variation of pure nc-Hodge structures over $S$ is a triple $(H, \mathcal{E}_B, \mathbf{iso})$ where
  • $H$ is a holomorphic $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded vector bundle on $\mathbb{A}^1 \times S$ which is algebraic in the $\mathbb{A}^1$-direction.
  • $\mathcal{E}_B$ is a local system of $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces on $(\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\})\times S$.
  • $\mathbf{iso}$ is an analytic isomorphism of holomorphic vector bundles $$\mathbf{iso}\colon\mathcal{E}_B\otimes\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\})\times S}\xrightarrow{\simeq} H_{\vert (\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\})\times S}.$$
Let $\nabla$ be the induced meromorphic connection on $H$. The data $(H,\mathcal{E}_B,\mathbf{iso})$ should satisfy:
  • (nc-filtration axiom) The connection $\nabla$ has a regular singularity along $\{\infty\}\times S$ and Poincaré rank $\leq 1$ along $\{0\}\times S$, i.e. $$u^2\cdot\nabla_{\partial/\partial u}\colon H\to H$$is a holomorphic differential operator on $H$ of order $\leq 1$.
  • (Griffiths transversality axiom) For every locally defined vector field $\xi\in T_S$ we have $$u\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\colon H\to H$$is a holomorphic differential operator on $H$ of order $\leq 1$.
  • ($\mathbb{Q}$-structure axiom) The Stokes structure on the local system $\mathbf{S}$ on $\mathbb{S}^1\times S$ is well defined, i.e. the steps in the Deligne-Malgrange-Stokes filtration on $\mathbf{S}$ are sheaves on $\mathbb{S}^1\times S$. Furthermore, the $\mathbb{Q}$-structure $\mathcal{E}_B$ is compatible with the Stokes structure as in Definition 1.3.1.
  • (opposedness axiom) The relative version of the gluing construction for nc-Hodge structures gives a globally defined complex vector bundle $\hat{H}$ on $\mathbb{P}^1\times S$, which is holomorphically trivial in the $\mathbb{P}^1$ direction. Moreover, with respect to the extension $\hat{H}$ the connection $\nabla$ is meromorphic with Poincaré rank one along $(\{0\}\times S)\cup(\{\infty\}\times S)$.

Relation to usual Hodge theory.

Now we compare nc-Hodge structures with the usual (pure) Hodge structures. Following [PS08], we recall that

Definition. Let $V$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space. A pure $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure of weight $w\in\mathbb{Z}$ on $V$ is the data of a decreasing filtration $F^{\bullet} V$ on $V_{\mathbb{C}}:=V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}$ such that $$V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q=w} V^{p,q},$$where $V^{p,q}:=F^p V \cap \overline{F^q V}$ and $\overline{F^{\bullet} V}$ is the complex conjugation of $F^{\bullet} V$ with respect to the real structure $V_{\mathbb{R}}:=V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\subseteq V_{\mathbb{C}}$. We will write $(V_{\mathbb{C}},F^{\bullet} V,V_{\mathbb{Q}})$ for a pure $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structure.

We will write $(\mathbb{Q}\text{-HS})$ for the abelian $\mathbb{Q}$-linear tensor category of pure rational Hodge structures. For each $w\in\mathbb{Z}$ we have the Hodge structure of Tate, $\mathbb{Q}(w):=(\mathbb{C},F^{\bullet},\mathbb{Q})$ where $F^i =\mathbb{C}$ for $i\leq w$ and $F^i =0$ for $i>w$. The Hodge structure of Tate has weight $2w$.

Via a Rees module construction similar to [Simpson91], given a pure Hodge structure $(V,F^{\bullet} V,V_{\mathbb{Q}})$ of weight $w$ we can consider the rank one meromorphic bundle with connection $$\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}_{\frac{w}{2}}:=\left(\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}],d-\frac{w}{2}\cdot\frac{du}{u}\right)$$and we set

  • $\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}^{w \mod 2} :=\sum_i u^{-i} F^i V\{u\}$ viewed as a $\mathbb{C}\{u\}$-submodule in $\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}} V$. This submodule is clearly preserved by the operator $\nabla_{u\frac{d}{du}}$ for the connection $\nabla :=(d-\frac{w}{2}\cdot\frac{du}{u})\otimes\mathrm{id}_V$, i.e. $(\mathcal{H},\nabla)$ is a logarithmic holomorphic extension of the meromorphic bundle with connection $\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}_{\frac{w}{2}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}} V$.
  • $\mathcal{E}_B:=\mathcal{E}_B^{w\mod 2}$ - the $\mathbb{Q}$-local system on $\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\}$ defined as the subsheaf $\mathcal{E}_B\subseteq H$ consisting of sections whose value at $1$ is in $V_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq V=H/(u-1)H$. In other words $\mathcal{E}_B$ is the locally constant sheaf on $\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\}$ with fiber $V_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and monodromy $(-1)^w\mathrm{id}_{V_{\mathbb{Q}}}$.
  • $\mathbf{iso}$ is the isomorphism of complex local systems, corresponding to the embedding $\mathcal{E}_B\subseteq H$.

We can easily check that the triple constructed this way defines a nc-Hodge structure, and if $V=\mathbb{Q}(w)$, the corresponding nc-Hodge structures are all trivial.

Remark. In this case we can compute horizontal sections of $\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}_{\frac{w}{2}}$. It corresponds to solving the equation $$df-\frac{w}{2}\frac{du}{u}=0,$$so that in any simply connected open subsetes $U\subseteq\mathbb{A}^1\setminus\{0\}$ we get $f=u^{w/2}$.

The assignment $(V,F^{\bullet}V,V_{\mathbb{Q}})\to (H,\mathcal{E}_B,\mathbf{iso})$ gives a functor $$\mathfrak{n}\colon (\mathbb{Q}\text{-HS})\to(\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})$$which factors through the quotient $$\pi\colon (\mathbb{Q}\text{-HS})\to(\mathbb{Q}\text{-HS})/(\bullet\otimes\mathbb{Q}(1)).$$Write $\mathfrak{N}\colon (\mathbb{Q}\text{-HS})/(\bullet\otimes\mathbb{Q}(1))\to (\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})$ for the resulting functor.

Lemma. The functor $\mathfrak{N}$ is fully faithful and its essential image consists of all nc-Hodge structures with regular singularities and monodromy $=\mathrm{id}$ on $H^0$ and $-\mathrm{id}$ on $H^1$.

ncHodge structures of exponential type.

Away from ncHodge structures coming from pure Hodge structures, there're ncHodge structures where the connection $\nabla$ has a pole at $0$ of order $2$. In this case, Theorem 1.2.3 applies and we know that for some ramification $\pi_p (t):=t^p=u$, the pull-back bundle $\pi_p^{\ast} (\mathcal{H},\nabla)[t^{-1}]$ has a formal decomposition into a direct sum of regular singular connections on meromorphic bundles multiplied by exponents of Laurent polynomials. Note that the bundle $\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}$ has non-vanishing horizontal section, if $\alpha =f\otimes dt$, given analytically by $e^f$.

As ncHodge structures only have singularities at $0$ of order at most $2$, it's possible that we don't need a quadratic ramification to get the decomposition. For instance, let $g_i (u)=c_i/u$, $c_i\in\mathbb{C}$, be distinct eigenvalues of the matrix $A_{-2}$, we define

Definition. An nc-Hodge structure $(H,\mathcal{E}_B,\mathbf{iso})$ is of exponential type if there exists a formal isomorphism $$\Psi\colon (\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\{u\}}\mathbb{C}[[u]],\nabla)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\bigoplus_{i=1}^m\left(\mathcal{E}^{c_i/u}\otimes (\mathcal{R}_i,\nabla_i)\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\{u\}}\mathbb{C}[[u]],$$where $(\mathcal{R}_i,\nabla_i)$ are meromorphic bundles with connections with regular singularities and $c_1,\dotsb, c_m\in\mathbb{C}$ denote the distinct eigenvalues of $A_{-2}$.
Remark.
  • There are various sufficient conditions guaranteeing a given nc-Hodge structure is decomposable without base change. For example, it's true if $A_{-2}$ has distinct eigenvalues or $A_{-1}=0$. More generally, it suffices to require that we can find holomorphic functions $\ell_i (u)\in\mathbb{C}\{u\}$ so that $\ell_i (0)=c_i$ for $i=1,\dotsb, m$ and the characteristic polynomial of $u^2A(u)$ is $\det (c\cdot\mathrm{id} -u^2 A(u))=\prod_{i=1}^m (c-\ell_i)^{\nu_i}$.
  • Not every irregular connection with a pole of order two is of exponential type. Indeed, the rank two connection $$\nabla=d-\begin{pmatrix} 0&u^{-2}\\ u^{-1}&\frac{u^{-1}}{2} \end{pmatrix}$$has a holomorphic section $$\begin{pmatrix}e^{-2u^{-1/2}}\\u^{1/2}e^{-2u^{-1/2}}\end{pmatrix}$$and so we need a quadratic base change for the formal decomposition to work for this connection.
  • If an nc-Hodge structure $(H,\mathcal{E}_B,\mathbf{iso})$ is of exponential type, then one can check that for each $i=1,2,\dotsb, m$ we can find a unique holomorphic extension $\mathcal{H}_{c_i}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_i$ in which the connection has a second order pole and so that $\Psi$ induces a formal isomorphism of holomorphic bundles with meromorphic connections $$\Psi\colon (\mathcal{H},\nabla)\otimes\mathbb{C}[[u]]\xrightarrow{\simeq}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^m\mathcal{E}^{c_i/u}\bigotimes_{\mathbb{C}\{u\}} (\mathcal{H}_{c_i},\nabla_i)\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[[u]]$$over $\mathbb{C}[[u]]$.

We expect that for nc-Hodge structures arising in mirror symmetry, they should always be of exponential type. We will go back to this point later. Let's end this section with the following Lemma.

Lemma. The categories $(\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})^{\mathrm{reg}}\subseteq (\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})^{\mathrm{exp}}\subseteq (\mathbb{Q}\text{-ncHS})$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linear abelian categories. The respective categories of polarizable nc-Hodge structures are semi-simple.
